VOLUNTEER POLICY


PREAMBLE
Bhavya Foundation recognizes that volunteerism is an expression of civic responsibility and public service.
Volunteers are essential partners in advancing the Foundation's mission of environmental conservation, education, heritage preservation, and community development.
This Policy establishes the framework for recruiting, supporting, supervising, recognizing, and protecting volunteers while ensuring that volunteer activities are conducted safely, ethically, and in accordance with the Foundation's values and applicable law.

ARTICLE 1
PURPOSE
The objectives of this Policy are to:
Create a structured volunteer programme.
Ensure fair and transparent recruitment.
Protect volunteers and beneficiaries.
Promote safety and professionalism.
Encourage long-term volunteer engagement.
Support leadership development.
Build a culture of service.

ARTICLE 2
THE BHAVYA VOLUNTEER CORPS
The Foundation shall establish the Bhavya Volunteer Corps (BVC) as its official volunteer network.
The Corps exists to:
support Foundation programmes;
promote community participation;
develop future leaders;
strengthen environmental stewardship; and
encourage lifelong service.
Membership in the Corps does not create an employment relationship.

ARTICLE 3
ELIGIBILITY
A person may volunteer if they:
support the Foundation's mission;
agree to follow the Constitution and policies;
complete the required registration process;
satisfy any programme-specific eligibility requirements; and
where required, provide parental or guardian consent if they are a minor.
Additional screening may be required for roles involving children, finances, or sensitive responsibilities.

ARTICLE 4
RECRUITMENT
Volunteers may be recruited through:
schools;
colleges and universities;
Panchayats;
community organizations;
social media;
Foundation events;
referrals; and
public campaigns.
Recruitment shall be inclusive and based on the needs of the Foundation.

ARTICLE 5
REGISTRATION
Every volunteer shall complete a Volunteer Registration Form containing:
Full Name
Date of Birth
Address
Contact Details
Emergency Contact
Skills
Areas of Interest
Availability
Relevant Experience
Declaration of Accuracy
Consent to Applicable Policies
The Foundation shall maintain secure volunteer records.

ARTICLE 6
ORIENTATION
Before commencing service, volunteers shall receive an orientation covering:
the Foundation's mission and values;
the Constitution;
Code of Ethics;
Child Protection Policy;
Health and Safety requirements;
role expectations;
reporting procedures; and
use of Foundation property and resources.
Programme-specific training shall be provided where necessary.

ARTICLE 7
RIGHTS OF VOLUNTEERS
Volunteers have the right to:
be treated with dignity and respect;
receive appropriate orientation and guidance;
work in a safe environment;
know the expectations of their role;
raise concerns without retaliation;
receive recognition for meaningful service; and
have their personal information protected in accordance with applicable law.

ARTICLE 8
RESPONSIBILITIES OF VOLUNTEERS
Volunteers shall:
uphold the Foundation's Constitution and policies;
act honestly and respectfully;
protect Foundation property;
maintain confidentiality where appropriate;
follow lawful instructions related to their role;
respect beneficiaries, fellow volunteers, and staff;
avoid conflicts of interest;
promptly report safety concerns or misconduct.
Volunteers shall represent the Foundation professionally at all times while engaged in Foundation activities.

ARTICLE 9
SAFEGUARDING
Where volunteers work with children or vulnerable persons, they shall:
comply with the Foundation's Child Protection Policy;
maintain appropriate professional boundaries;
never engage in abusive, exploitative, or inappropriate conduct;
report safeguarding concerns immediately.
The safety and dignity of beneficiaries take precedence over all other considerations.

ARTICLE 10
HEALTH & SAFETY
The Foundation shall take reasonable steps to provide a safe volunteer environment.
Volunteers shall:
follow safety instructions;
use protective equipment where provided;
report accidents or hazards promptly; and
refrain from activities for which they have not been trained or authorized.
Emergency procedures shall be communicated before major field activities.

ARTICLE 11
EXPENSES
Volunteer service is ordinarily unpaid.
Where approved by the Foundation, reasonable out-of-pocket expenses directly incurred in performing authorized volunteer duties may be reimbursed in accordance with the Financial Management Policy.
Reimbursement does not constitute wages or employment.

ARTICLE 12
IDENTIFICATION
The Foundation may issue volunteer identification cards, badges, T-shirts, or other identification materials.
Such items remain the property of the Foundation and shall be returned upon request or at the conclusion of volunteer service.

ARTICLE 13
PERFORMANCE & DEVELOPMENT
The Foundation encourages volunteers to develop their knowledge and skills.
Opportunities may include:
training programmes;
workshops;
environmental education;
AI literacy sessions;
leadership development;
mentoring; and
certificates of participation.
Volunteer development supports both individual growth and institutional capacity.

ARTICLE 14
RECOGNITION
The Foundation values meaningful service.
Recognition may include:
certificates;
letters of appreciation;
awards;
public acknowledgement (with consent);
leadership opportunities; and
recommendations where appropriate.
Recognition shall reflect contribution rather than popularity.

ARTICLE 15
DISCIPLINE
Where a volunteer fails to comply with the Foundation's Constitution, policies, or Code of Ethics, the Foundation may take appropriate action, including:
guidance or counseling;
additional training;
temporary suspension from volunteer duties;
removal from the Volunteer Corps.
Before significant action is taken, the volunteer should ordinarily be informed of the concerns and given an opportunity to respond, consistent with fairness and the seriousness of the matter.

ARTICLE 16
GRIEVANCES
Volunteers may raise concerns regarding:
unsafe conditions;
harassment;
discrimination;
misconduct;
unfair treatment;
ethical concerns; or
programme issues.
The Foundation shall review grievances fairly, respectfully, and without retaliation.

ARTICLE 17
END OF VOLUNTEER SERVICE
Volunteer service may conclude through:
voluntary resignation;
completion of the agreed role;
inactivity for an extended period;
programme completion; or
removal in accordance with Foundation policies.
The Foundation may conduct an exit discussion to gather feedback and identify opportunities for improvement.

ARTICLE 18
VOLUNTEER LEADERSHIP
The Foundation may establish leadership roles within the Bhavya Volunteer Corps, including:
Team Leader
Project Coordinator
District Volunteer Coordinator
State Volunteer Coordinator
National Volunteer Coordinator
Leadership appointments shall be based on demonstrated commitment, capability, integrity, and service.

ARTICLE 19
POLICY REVIEW
This Policy shall be reviewed at least every two years or earlier where:
programme needs evolve;
legal requirements change; or
the Board determines revision is necessary.
