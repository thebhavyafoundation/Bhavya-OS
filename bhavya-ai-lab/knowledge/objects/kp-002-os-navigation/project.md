# Project: OS Navigation Guide — Portfolio Edition

**Knowledge Package:** KP-002
**Duration:** 1 week (in-class + homework)
**Grade Level:** 9
**Subject:** Digital Literacy

---

## Project Overview

Create a **publishable OS Navigation Guide** — a portfolio-quality resource you can share on GitHub, present to your class, and be proud of. This isn't just an assignment. It's a resource that could actually help a new computer user get started.

Your guide will demonstrate mastery of desktop navigation, window management, system settings, Task Manager, and keyboard shortcuts — organized as a professional reference document with real screenshots, clear instructions, and thoughtful reflections.

---

## Learning Objectives

1. Synthesize all KP-002 concepts into a cohesive, publishable resource
2. Practice technical writing and instructional design
3. Create a GitHub-ready project with proper documentation
4. Demonstrate understanding through real-world examples and screenshots
5. Reflect on your learning journey with depth and honesty

---

## Deliverables

### 1. GitHub-Ready Guide (Main Deliverable)

Your guide must be structured like a professional GitHub repository README. It must include:

#### Required Sections

**Title and Introduction**
- Clear, descriptive title (e.g., "The Student's Guide to OS Navigation")
- A 2-3 sentence description of what this guide covers and who it's for
- At least one annotated screenshot showing the complete desktop

**Desktop Interface Section**
- Labeled screenshot of YOUR desktop with every component annotated (taskbar, Start menu, system tray, icons, notification area)
- Step-by-step instructions for finding each component
- Tips for organizing a clean desktop
- At least 1 "common mistake" callout (e.g., "Don't put 50 icons on your desktop")

**Window Management Section**
- Screenshots showing: minimized window, maximized window, side-by-side arrangement
- Step-by-step instructions for: moving, resizing, minimizing, maximizing, closing, Snap/split-screen
- Troubleshooting table with 3 common problems and solutions
- At least 1 keyboard shortcut highlighted (Alt+Tab)

**System Settings Section**
- Screenshots of 3 different settings screens from YOUR computer
- Instructions for: changing wallpaper, adjusting brightness, checking privacy settings, connecting to Wi-Fi
- A "Privacy Checklist" — 5 things every student should check
- Security recommendation callout box

**Keyboard Shortcuts Reference**
- Table of at least 15 shortcuts organized by category (Editing, Navigation, System)
- Windows AND macOS equivalents for each shortcut
- At least 3 "practice exercises" for memorizing shortcuts
- A "Top 5 Most Useful" highlight section

**Task Manager Section**
- Screenshot of YOUR Task Manager with key areas labeled
- Instructions for: opening Task Manager, reading CPU/memory, closing a frozen program
- Safety warning box: "Never close processes you don't recognize"
- A real-world scenario: "Your friend's computer is slow. Walk them through Task Manager."

**Reflection Section**
- What surprised you most about this topic
- Which skill was hardest and how you overcame it
- How you plan to use these skills in other subjects
- Advice for a future student learning this material

**How I Built This**
- What tools you used to create the guide
- What you learned about technical writing
- What you would do differently next time

## Quality Standards

### Writing Quality
- Clear, concise language (write for a Grade 9 audience)
- No jargon without explanation
- Active voice: "Drag the window to the edge" not "The window can be dragged"
- Step-by-step instructions numbered and testable

### Screenshot Quality
- Screenshots must be from YOUR actual computer (not stock images)
- Each screenshot must have labels, arrows, or highlights showing key elements
- Screenshots must be large enough to read (minimum 1280x720)
- Consistent annotation style across all screenshots

### Presentation Quality
- Consistent heading hierarchy
- Tables for reference information
- Callout boxes for warnings, tips, and key insights
- Proper markdown formatting

## Tips for Success

1. **Take screenshots first** — Write around them, not the other way around
2. **Test every instruction** — If you can't follow your own steps, rewrite them
3. **Use your own words** — Explain things as YOU understand them
4. **Get feedback** — Have a classmate follow your instructions. Where do they get stuck?
5. **Polish the details** — Consistent formatting, proper spelling, working links
