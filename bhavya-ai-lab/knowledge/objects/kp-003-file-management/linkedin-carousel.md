## Slide 3: Rule 1 — Be Descriptive

**Headline:** Rule 1: Be Descriptive

**Body:**
- Bad: `doc1.txt`
- Good: `science-lab-report.pdf`

Your file name should tell you what is inside without opening it. If you cannot figure out the content from the name, it is a bad name.

**Visual:** Before/after comparison with red X on bad name, green check on good name

**Speaker Notes:** The most fundamental rule. Emphasize that descriptive names save time not just for the creator but for anyone who accesses the file.

---

## Slide 4: Rule 2 — Date First

**Headline:** Rule 2: Date First (YYYY-MM-DD)

**Body:**
- Bad: `report-aug-5.docx`
- Good: `2026-08-05_report.docx`

Putting the date at the beginning makes files sort chronologically. Your most recent work always appears at the top of any list.

**Visual:** Calendar icon with date format highlighted

**Speaker Notes:** Explain that YYYY-MM-DD is an international standard. It works because year-month-day sorts correctly in any file system. Month-day-year does not.

---

## Slide 5: Rule 3 — No Spaces

**Headline:** Rule 3: No Spaces — Use Hyphens

**Body:**
- Bad: `my science report.docx`
- Good: `my-science-report.docx`

Spaces cause errors on web servers, in scripts, and across operating systems. Hyphens and underscores work everywhere.

**Visual:** Hyphen icon with example transformation

**Speaker Notes:** This is a technical rule with practical implications. Students who learn this early avoid headaches when they work with web development, programming, or cross-platform collaboration.

---

## Slide 6: Rule 4 — No Special Characters

**Headline:** Rule 4: No Special Characters

**Body:**
- Forbidden: / \ : * ? " < > |
- Bad: `report!.pdf`
- Good: `report-v2.pdf`

Special characters cause errors in file systems, URLs, and programs. Stick to letters, numbers, hyphens, and underscores.

**Visual:** Warning icon with forbidden characters listed

**Speaker Notes:** Some characters have special meanings in operating systems and programming. Teaching students to avoid them prevents technical problems later.

---

## Slide 7: Rule 5 — Short But Clear

**Headline:** Rule 5: Short But Clear

**Body:**
- Bad: `my really long presentation that I worked on for hours.pptx`
- Good: `science-presentation-final.pptx`

Aim for 3-8 words. Long names get truncated in file explorers and are hard to read at a glance.

**Visual:** Text truncation example showing a long name cut off

**Speaker Notes:** Balance between descriptiveness and brevity. The best file names are concise but unmistakably clear about the content.

---

## Slide 8: Folder Organization

**Headline:** Organize with Folders

**Body:**
Three methods that work:

**By Subject:** School/Science/Lab-Reports
**By Project:** Projects/Science-Fair/Research
**By Date:** 2026/August/Week-3

The golden rule: Create folders before you need them.

**Visual:** Tree structure diagram showing folder hierarchy

**Speaker Notes:** Folders are the shelves of your digital library. Without them, files pile up on the desktop like books on the floor. Present all three methods and explain that the best choice depends on the user's workflow.

---

## Slide 9: The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

**Headline:** Protect Your Work

**Body:**
The 3-2-1 Backup Rule:

- **3** copies of important files
- **2** different types of storage
- **1** copy offsite (cloud storage)

Free options: Google Drive (15 GB), OneDrive (5 GB), iCloud (5 GB)

Your work is worth protecting.

**Visual:** Three-circle diagram showing the 3-2-1 rule

**Speaker Notes:** Backups are not optional. Hard drives fail, computers get stolen, files get accidentally deleted. The 3-2-1 rule is simple, effective, and accessible to every student with a free cloud account.

## Carousel Description (for LinkedIn post)

File management is one of those skills that schools rarely teach but every student needs. The average student wastes 30 minutes per week searching for files — that is 18 hours per school year.

Here are 5 rules that take 20 minutes to learn and save hours every month:

1. Be descriptive — name files so you know what is inside
2. Date first — use YYYY-MM-DD for automatic chronological sorting
3. No spaces — use hyphens instead
4. No special characters — avoid / \ : * ? " < > |
5. Short but clear — 3-8 words that describe the content

Plus: organize with folders (by subject, project, or date) and protect your work with the 3-2-1 backup rule (3 copies, 2 storage types, 1 offsite).

These are professional skills that serve students for life. Apply them today.
