# Learning Evidence

## What Evidence Proves the Student Learned?

### 1. Prompt Improvement

**Current:** Student writes two prompts (first attempt and improved version).
**Problem:** We don't show the difference in output. The student doesn't SEE that the improved prompt works better.
**Recommendation:** Show AI responses to both prompts side-by-side.

### 2. Reflection Quality

**Current:** Student answers two questions: "What surprised you?" and "What would you do differently?"
**Problem:** Reflections are stored in localStorage but never reviewed or analyzed.
**Recommendation:** Analyze reflection content for depth and insight. Show the student what good reflection looks like.

### 3. Iteration Quality

**Current:** Student can retry experiments in the playground.
**Problem:** Most students will try once, see a bad score, and move on. The retry flow is not prominent.
**Recommendation:** Make retry the default flow. Show improvement as a journey.

### 4. Portfolio Quality

**Current:** Student gets a title, summary, skills badges, and their prompt text.
**Problem:** This is not evidence of learning. It's evidence of typing.
**Recommendation:** Include AI responses, before/after comparison, and reflection content.

### 5. Reasoning Quality

**Current:** No evidence of reasoning.
**Problem:** We don't know if the student understands WHY the improved prompt works better.
**Recommendation:** Add a "Why did your improved prompt work better?" question.

---

## What We're Currently Measuring (Wrong)

### Time Spent

**Measuring:** How long the student spent on each screen.
**Problem:** Time spent ≠ learning. A student could spend 10 minutes confused.

### Pages Viewed

**Measuring:** Which screens the student visited.
**Problem:** Visiting screens ≠ understanding content.

### Completion

**Measuring:** Whether the student clicked "Complete Lesson."
**Problem:** Clicking a button ≠ learning something.

---

## What We Should Be Measuring

### 1. Prompt Quality Progression

Track the student's prompt scores across attempts:

- Attempt 1: 20/100
- Attempt 2: 60/100
- Attempt 3: 80/100
  **Evidence:** The student improved from 20 to 80. That's learning.

### 2. Reflection Depth

Analyze the student's reflections:

- Surface: "It was okay"
- Medium: "I learned to be specific"
- Deep: "I realized that the audience matters because AI generates different responses for different people"
  **Evidence:** The student can articulate what they learned.

### 3. Output Comparison

Show the AI's responses to the first and improved prompts:

- First prompt → generic response
- Improved prompt → specific, useful response
  **Evidence:** The student can SEE the difference their improvement made.

### 4. Transfer Ability

Can the student apply the skill to a new situation?

- Prompt: "Now explain photosynthesis to a 10-year-old"
  **Evidence:** The student can generalize from the lesson to a new context.

### 5. Teaching Ability

Can the student explain what they learned to someone else?

- Prompt: "Write a tweet explaining what you learned about prompting AI"
  **Evidence:** The student can teach, which is the highest form of learning.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Show Prompt Score Progression

After each attempt, show: "Attempt 1: 20/100 → Attempt 2: 60/100 → Attempt 3: 80/100"

### 2. Show AI Response Comparison

After the improved prompt, show:
"Your first prompt got this response: [weak response]
Your improved prompt got this response: [strong response]"

### 3. Analyze Reflections

Grade reflections on depth and provide feedback: "Your reflection shows deep understanding. You connected the lesson to real-world applications."

### 4. Add Transfer Task

After the main experiment, add: "Now try explaining a different topic using what you learned."

### 5. Add Teaching Task

Add: "Write a one-sentence tip for someone who wants to learn prompt engineering."
