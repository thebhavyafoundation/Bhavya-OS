## Too Many Concepts?

### Concepts Introduced

1. AI (expected)
2. Prompt (new jargon)
3. Scoring (new concept)
4. Mentor (new concept)
5. Reflection (expected)
6. Portfolio (new concept)
7. Artifact (new jargon)

**Verdict:** 7 concepts is too many for a first lesson. Especially when 3 are new jargon (prompt, artifact, scoring).

---

## Too Many Choices?

### Choices the Student Must Make

1. What to type as their first prompt (open-ended)
2. What to write in reflection (open-ended)
3. What to type as their improved prompt (open-ended)
4. Which emoji to click (4 options)
5. Whether to copy or download (2 options)

**Verdict:** 5 choices is acceptable. The open-ended ones might be overwhelming for some beginners.

---

## Too Many Instructions?

### Instructions per Screen

- Welcome: 3 instructions (good)
- First Prompt: 1 instruction (good)
- Reflect: 2 instructions (good)
- Build: 1 instruction + 4 checklist items (medium)
- Portfolio: 0 instructions (good)

**Verdict:** Good. Instructions are minimal and clear.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Remove Jargon

Replace "prompt" with "message" in the first interaction.
Replace "portfolio artifact" with "something you can share."

### 2. Reduce Concepts

Don't introduce "scoring" in the first lesson. Just show the AI response improving.

### 3. Add Examples

Show an example prompt before asking the student to write one.

### 4. Simplify Reflection

Instead of 2 open-ended questions, ask: "What would you do differently next time?" (one question).

### 5. Keep the Checklist

The checklist is excellent. It reduces cognitive load by giving clear targets.

---

## Score: 7/10

The cognitive load is generally good. The main issue is jargon (prompt, artifact, scoring) that could confuse beginners.
