# Comparison Experience

## The Before & After View

The comparison is the core of the Aha! moment. The student must see the difference between their first and improved attempts.

## What Is Shown

### Before (Left Side)

- First prompt text
- AI's response to first prompt
- Quality label: "Vague" / "Basic"

### After (Right Side)

- Improved prompt text
- AI's response to improved prompt
- Quality label: "Clear" / "Specific" / "Excellent"

### What Changed (Below)

- List of specific improvements:
  - "Added more detail and context"
  - "Specified who this is for"
  - "Added a specific format or structure"
  - "Made the message more detailed"

## When It Appears

The comparison appears after:

1. The student has made at least 2 attempts
2. The student clicks "Compare your first attempt vs latest"

## Why It Matters

### 1. Visible Improvement

The student doesn't just "feel" like they improved. They SEE it.

### 2. Clear Cause and Effect

The student can see exactly what they changed and how it affected the AI's response.

### 3. Learning Reinforcement

The comparison reinforces the learning: "Being specific produces better results."

### 4. Motivation

Seeing improvement motivates the student to continue.

## Design Rules

### 1. Side-by-Side

Show both attempts on the same screen. Don't make the student scroll or navigate.

### 2. Highlight Differences

Make the differences obvious. Use color, labels, or visual cues.

### 3. Explain Changes

Don't just show the changes — explain why they matter.

### 4. Celebrate Progress

The comparison should feel like a celebration, not a judgment.
