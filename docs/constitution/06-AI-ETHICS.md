# Constitution 06 — AI Ethics

**Document Number:** 06  
**Title:** AI Ethics Constitution  
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL  
**Effective Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Authority:** Derived from Document 00 (Vision), Articles 2, 5, and 10  
**Supersedes:** All conflicting AI policies, machine learning guidelines, and automation ethics documents

---

## Preamble

AI is a tool to serve the mission, not the mission itself. Bhavya Foundation uses artificial intelligence to accelerate education, content creation, and institutional operations. AI does not make decisions about people. AI does not replace human judgment. AI does not operate without oversight.

This constitution establishes the ethical boundaries within which all AI systems must operate. These boundaries are constitutional law — not guidelines, not recommendations, not aspirational values. Law.

---

## Article 1 — The Primacy of Human Judgment

### 1.1 AI as Tool, Not Mission

Bhavya Foundation's mission is to restore nature, advance knowledge, preserve heritage, and empower communities. AI is one of many tools that serve this mission. AI is not the mission. No AI capability is built, acquired, or deployed unless it demonstrably advances one of these four pillars.

**Enforcement:** Every AI deployment proposal must declare which mission pillar it serves. Proposals that cannot declare a mission pillar are rejected.

### 1.2 Human Override is Absolute

No AI system within Bhavya Foundation may make final decisions about people, content publication, institutional policy, or financial allocation without explicit human approval. AI proposes. Humans dispose.

**Enforcement:** All AI-generated outputs that affect people or institutional state require human review before execution. Automated publication without human approval is a constitutional violation.

### 1.3 No Autonomous Decision-Making About People

AI systems may not:

- Automatically accept or reject applicants
- Automatically grade student work without human review
- Automatically terminate accounts or access
- Automatically make hiring, placement, or recommendation decisions about individuals
- Automatically assess student performance for high-stakes outcomes

**Enforcement:** Any AI system that makes autonomous decisions about individuals is suspended immediately and reviewed against this article.

## Article 2 — Transparency About AI Usage

### 2.1 Disclosure Requirement

Every publication, publication pipeline, educational asset, or institutional output that involves AI generation, AI assistance, or AI processing must be transparently disclosed.

**Required disclosure format:**

```
This [content type] was produced with AI assistance.
Human review: [Reviewer name], [Date]
AI contribution: [Description of AI role]
```

### 2.2 AI Role Clarity

AI usage must be classified:

| Category              | Definition                                       | Disclosure Required         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| **AI Generated**      | Content created by AI, reviewed by human         | Yes — full disclosure       |
| **AI Assisted**       | Content created by human with AI help            | Yes — partial disclosure    |
| **AI Reviewed**       | Content created by human, checked by AI          | No — AI is QA tool          |
| **AI Infrastructure** | AI powers backend systems, no user-facing output | Internal documentation only |

### 2.3 No Deceptive AI

AI must never be presented as human. AI-generated content must never be attributed to a human author without explicit disclosure. AI must never impersonate a person, an institution, or a human process.

**Enforcement:** Any AI system that deceives users about its nature is a constitutional violation. This is a hard fail — no override permitted.

### 2.4 AI in Brand Communications

AI-generated visual content, copy, or media for brand purposes must:

- Follow the Brand Constitution (Document 01) without exception
- Be reviewed by the brand governance authority
- Never use AI to fabricate statistics, testimonials, or impact claims
- Never use AI-generated imagery that presents fictional scenarios as real impact

## Article 3 — No AI-Generated Content Without Human Review

### 3.1 The Human Review Gate

Every piece of content that is published under Bhavya Foundation's name — whether generated by AI, assisted by AI, or merely reviewed by AI — must pass through human review before publication.

**This includes:**

- Knowledge Packages
- Lessons, labs, assessments
- Website articles and pages
- Social media content
- Governance documents
- Educational materials
- Newsletters, videos, podcasts

**This does not include:**

- Automated system logs
- Internal diagnostics
- Cache invalidation messages
- Routine infrastructure notifications

### 3.2 Review Standards

Human reviewers must verify:

| Check                    | Description                                                      | Authority                 |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| **Accuracy**             | All facts, definitions, and claims are correct                   | Content Constitution (03) |
| **Brand Consistency**    | Voice, tone, visual identity comply with Brand Constitution (01) | Brand Constitution        |
| **Cultural Sensitivity** | No stereotyping, appropriation, or disrespectful framing         | Vision (00), Article 2    |
| **Mission Alignment**    | Content serves at least one mission pillar                       | Vision (00), Article 2    |
| **AI Disclosure**        | AI usage is properly disclosed per Article 2.1                   | This Constitution         |
| **Originality**          | Content does not plagiarize or misattribute sources              | Content Constitution (03) |
| **Citation**             | Every claim has a verifiable source                              | Content Constitution (03) |

### 3.3 Review Accountability

The human reviewer is accountable for the content they approve. The reviewer's name, review date, and review scope are logged. AI-generated content without a named human reviewer is considered unpublished.

### 3.4 Emergency Publication

In exceptional circumstances where publication is time-critical and no human reviewer is available:

1. AI may prepare the content for publication
2. The content is flagged as "Emergency Publication — Pending Review"
3. A human reviewer must be assigned within 24 hours
4. If no reviewer is assigned within 24 hours, the content is automatically retracted
5. Emergency publications may not exceed 2 per quarter

## Article 4 — Child Protection

### 4.1 Scope

Bhavya Foundation serves learners of all ages, including minors. Any AI system that interacts with, collects data from, or generates content for users under the age of 18 must comply with this article.

### 4.2 Age-Appropriate AI Interactions

| Age Group | AI Interaction Rules                              | Data Collection                              |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Under 13  | No direct AI interaction without parental consent | Minimal — name and learning progress only    |
| 13-15     | Limited AI interaction with parental notification | Name, learning progress, content preferences |
| 16-17     | AI interaction permitted with parental awareness  | Standard learning data per privacy policy    |
| 18+       | Full AI interaction                               | Standard learning data per privacy policy    |

### 4.3 Prohibited AI Behaviors with Minors

AI systems must never:

- Collect personal information from minors beyond learning data
- Display age-inappropriate content
- Use manipulative or persuasive language with minors
- Encourage minors to share personal information
- Interact with minors through channels not approved by parents/guardians
- Use emotional profiling or behavioral manipulation
- Operate without clear, age-appropriate explanations of AI capabilities

### 4.4 Consent Mechanisms

For minors:

1. Parental/guardian consent must be obtained before AI interaction
2. The consent must explain what data is collected, how AI is used, and what rights the parent/guardian has
3. Consent may be withdrawn at any time
4. Withdrawal of consent triggers immediate data deletion

### 4.5 Safeguarding Enforcement

Any AI system that violates child protection rules is immediately suspended. The violation is reported to the governance board within 24 hours. The governance board may:

- Require remediation before the system is re-enabled
- Require external review by a child protection expert
- Retire the system permanently if the violation is severe

## Article 5 — Environmental Awareness

### 5.1 Compute Cost Awareness

AI systems consume energy. Bhavya Foundation recognizes that every API call, every training run, every inference has an environmental cost. This cost must be accounted for.

**Requirements:**

1. All AI deployments must log compute usage (tokens, API calls, GPU hours)
2. Quarterly environmental impact reports must be produced
3. Compute budget must be approved by the governance board annually
4. No AI system may use more compute than necessary for its mission-critical function

### 5.2 Efficiency Mandate

When evaluating AI solutions, teams must consider:

- Smaller models that achieve acceptable performance over larger models
- Batch processing over real-time when real-time is not required
- Caching to avoid redundant computation
- Local inference over cloud inference when feasible

### 5.3 Carbon Transparency

Publications that discuss AI capabilities must be honest about environmental costs. No AI-related content may claim AI is environmentally free or costless. Transparency about compute costs is a constitutional requirement.

### 5.4 Infrastructure Choices

When choosing AI infrastructure, preference order is:

1. Local/open-source models when they meet requirements
2. Cloud providers with renewable energy commitments
3. Cloud providers with carbon offset programs
4. Other cloud providers (with documented justification)

## Article 6 — Data Privacy

### 6.1 Data Minimization

AI systems within Bhavya Foundation collect only the data they need to function. No unnecessary data collection. No speculative data collection. No "we might need this later" data collection.

**Enforcement:** Every data field collected by an AI system must be justified in the system's data impact assessment. Fields without justification are removed.

### 6.2 Purpose Limitation

Data collected for one purpose may not be used for another purpose without explicit consent. Learning data collected for curriculum adaptation may not be used for marketing. Engagement data collected for content improvement may not be used for behavioral profiling.

### 6.3 Storage and Retention

| Data Type            | Retention Period                 | Disposal Method               |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Learning progress    | Duration of enrollment + 1 year  | Anonymization                 |
| Assessment results   | Duration of enrollment + 2 years | Anonymization                 |
| Content interaction  | 90 days                          | Automatic deletion            |
| AI conversation logs | 30 days                          | Automatic deletion            |
| Behavioral analytics | 1 year                           | Anonymization                 |
| Personal identifiers | Duration of enrollment           | Deletion upon account closure |

### 6.4 Data Rights

Every user has the right to:

1. Access all data an AI system holds about them
2. Correct inaccurate data
3. Delete their data (subject to legitimate institutional needs)
4. Export their data in a standard format
5. Opt out of AI-driven personalization while retaining platform access
6. Understand how AI uses their data (plain-language explanation)

### 6.5 No Shadow Profiles

AI systems must not create profiles about users who have not explicitly consented to data collection. No inference about non-users. No behavioral prediction based on data from other users.

## Article 7 — Bias Awareness and Mitigation

### 7.1 Bias Recognition

All AI systems within Bhavya Foundation operate in contexts where bias exists. This is not theoretical — bias in education, content, and community is documented and measurable. Every AI system must acknowledge this reality.

### 7.2 Bias Assessment Requirements

Before deployment, every AI system must undergo:

| Assessment                     | Description                                                                             | Frequency         |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Training data audit**        | Review training data for representation gaps, historical bias, and skewed distributions | Before deployment |
| **Output audit**               | Test AI outputs across demographic groups for differential quality                      | Quarterly         |
| **Content bias review**        | Review AI-generated educational content for cultural, gender, and regional bias         | Per content batch |
| **Recommendation bias review** | Review recommendation systems for self-reinforcing loops                                | Monthly           |

### 7.3 Bias Mitigation Strategies

When bias is identified:

1. **Document** the bias with evidence and scope
2. **Assess** the impact on affected communities
3. **Implement** corrective measures (data augmentation, output filtering, model selection)
4. **Verify** that corrective measures reduce bias without introducing new bias
5. **Monitor** for regression

### 7.4 Indian Context Sensitivity

AI systems serving Bhavya Foundation must account for:

- Regional, linguistic, and cultural diversity across India
- Socioeconomic diversity in user populations
- Language diversity (not all users are English-native)
- Educational background diversity (not all users have formal technology education)
- Accessibility needs (visual, auditory, motor, cognitive)

### 7.5 Bias Escalation

If bias assessment reveals systemic bias that cannot be mitigated within current system design:

1. The system is flagged for governance board review
2. The governance board may require external audit
3. Continued deployment requires documented justification and monitoring plan
4. If bias cannot be mitigated, the system is retired

## Article 9 — AI Development Standards

### 9.1 Responsible AI Development

All AI development within Bhavya Foundation follows these principles:

1. **Start with the problem, not the technology.** AI is chosen because it solves a problem, not because it is new.
2. **Build for the least privileged user.** AI systems must work for users with the least access and the least technical knowledge.
3. **Fail safely.** AI failures must degrade gracefully, not catastrophically. When AI fails, the system should still function at reduced capacity.
4. **Log everything.** Every AI decision, every model inference, every data transformation must be logged for audit.
5. **Explain when asked.** AI systems must be able to explain why they made a recommendation, in plain language.

### 9.2 Model Governance

| Model Type                            | Governance Requirement                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Foundation models (GPT, Claude, etc.) | Governance board approval required                           |
| Fine-tuned models                     | Documented training data, bias assessment, governance review |
| Open-source models                    | Security audit, license compliance, governance approval      |
| Custom models                         | Full ML lifecycle governance, external audit recommended     |
| Prompt-based systems                  | Prompt documentation, output testing, governance review      |

### 9.3 AI System Inventory

All AI systems operating within Bhavya Foundation must be registered in the institutional registry with:

- Purpose and mission pillar alignment
- Data sources and data flows
- Bias assessment status
- Environmental impact estimate
- Human review process
- Fallback behavior when AI fails

### 9.4 Deprecation

AI systems that no longer serve the mission, exhibit unmitigable bias, or are superseded by better alternatives must be deprecated following the deprecation process in Document 02 (Bhavya OS Constitution), Article 5.4.

## Article 10 — What We Never Do with AI

These are absolute prohibitions. They cannot be overridden, justified, or excused by convenience, efficiency, or technical capability.

1. **Never make autonomous decisions about people.** AI proposes. Humans dispose. No exceptions.
2. **Never deceive users about AI's nature.** AI must always be transparent about what it is.
3. **Never fabricate data, statistics, or evidence.** AI-generated content must be verifiable.
4. **Never skip human review.** Every publication requires human accountability.
5. **Never use AI to manipulate.** No persuasive design, no emotional manipulation, no behavioral exploitation.
6. **Never collect unnecessary data.** Data minimization is not optional.
7. **Never deploy AI without bias assessment.** Bias is assumed; mitigation is required.
8. **Never present AI output as fact without verification.** AI hallucination is a known risk; verification is the mitigation.
9. **Never use AI to generate fake testimonials, endorsements, or social proof.** Authenticity is non-negotiable.
10. **Never deploy AI that cannot be audited.** Black-box systems that cannot explain their decisions are not used for institutional decisions.
11. **Never use AI to replace human community.** AI supplements human interaction; it does not replace mentorship, peer learning, or community building.
12. **Never optimize AI for engagement over learning.** Educational outcomes, not time-on-platform, are the measure of success.

## Article 11 — Enforcement and Accountability

### 11.1 Violation Categories

| Category     | Description                                              | Response                                                |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Minor**    | Disclosure omission, documentation gap                   | Correction within 48 hours                              |
| **Moderate** | Bias detected, privacy boundary crossed                  | System suspended, governance review                     |
| **Severe**   | Autonomous decision about people, deception, fabrication | System retired, incident report                         |
| **Critical** | Child protection violation, systematic manipulation      | Immediate suspension, external review, governance board |

### 11.2 Accountability Chain

```
AI System → System Owner → Domain Lead → Governance Board → Founder
```

Every AI system has a named owner who is accountable for its compliance with this constitution.

### 11.3 Audit Trail

All AI actions that affect people, content, or institutional state are logged with:

- Timestamp
- System identifier
- Action taken
- Input data reference
- Output produced
- Human reviewer (if applicable)

Audit logs are retained for 3 years and are accessible to the governance board.

### 11.4 Amendment Process

This constitution may only be amended through:

1. A written proposal with evidence supporting the change
2. Review by the governance board
3. A 30-day comment period
4. Approval by the institution builder designated for AI ethics
5. Publication with version bump and changelog entry

Amendments may only strengthen protections. Weakening of AI ethics protections requires extraordinary evidence and unanimous governance board approval.

## Article 12 — Definitions

For the purposes of this Constitution:

- **AI System** — Any software that uses machine learning, natural language processing, generative models, or algorithmic decision-making within Bhavya Foundation
- **AI Generated** — Content created primarily by AI with human review
- **AI Assisted** — Content created primarily by human with AI support
- **Human Review** — Verification by a named, accountable human who confirms the content meets quality standards
- **Bias** — Systematic unfairness in AI outputs that disadvantages specific groups
- **Data Minimization** — Collecting only the data strictly necessary for the AI system's declared purpose
- **Autonomous Decision** — An AI action that affects people, content, or institutional state without prior human approval
- **Child Protection** — Safeguards ensuring AI systems interacting with minors meet age-appropriate standards
- **Constitutional Validation** — Automated checking of publications against all 10 SDK rules before publication
- **Environmental Cost** — The energy consumption and carbon footprint of AI compute operations

## Article 13 — Effective Date and Authority

This Constitution takes effect on 2026-08-06 and remains in force indefinitely. It may only be amended through the process defined in Article 11.4.

The authority for this Constitution derives from Document 00 (Vision), Article 10 (Enforcement), which establishes that AI systems must cite constitutional authority, never override human judgment, log all actions, and refuse unconstitutional actions.
