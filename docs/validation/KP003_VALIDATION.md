## Beginner Walkthrough

### Time Spent
- Lesson: ~1 hour (as designed)
- Project: ~45 minutes (as designed)
- Assessment: ~30 minutes
- Lab: ~45 minutes
- **Total: ~3.25 hours direct engagement**

### Confusing Concepts
1. **Root and Path** — The "Map Your Computer's Brain" exercise is excellent, but the terms "root" and "path" may be abstract for students who have never thought about file system hierarchy.
2. **Copy vs. Move** — The distinction is explained clearly with the photocopy/tear analogy, but students often confuse Ctrl+X (Cut) with Copy in practice.
3. **3-2-1 Backup Rule** — The rule is memorable, but the explanation of "2 different types of storage" may confuse students who don't understand the difference between HDD, SSD, and cloud.
4. **File Extensions** — The concept that `report.docx` and `Report.DOCX` are different files on some systems is surprising and may not be fully understood.

### Questions Asked
1. "How do I find a file I saved?" (common student question)
2. "Why does file naming matter?" (addressed in Section 2)
3. "When should I use Copy vs. Move?" (addressed in Section 4)
4. "How do I organize my photos?" (addressed in Section 3)
5. "What is the 3-2-1 backup rule?" (addressed in Section 5)
6. "Can I undo a delete?" (addressed in Section 4)
7. "What happens if my computer breaks?" (addressed in Section 5)

### Points of Friction
1. **Opening Challenge** — "Can you find a file you saved more than two weeks ago in under 30 seconds?" This may frustrate students who have never organized their files. The30-second time pressure is aggressive.
2. **"Map Your Computer's Brain"** — The exercise asks students to "click to the highest level you can see." Beginners may not know how to navigate to the root level.
3. **File Naming Experiment** — "Create a file with a special character (like `!` or `#`). What happens?" On some systems, this works fine. On others, it may cause errors. The experiment may not produce consistent results.
4. **Storage Audit** — "How much total storage does your computer have?" Students may not know how to find this information.
5. **Full Challenge** — "You have 10 minutes. Complete this scenario from start to finish." 10 minutes is very tight for 5 steps including extracting, organizing, renaming, moving, and backing up.

### Moments of Excitement
1. **Opening Challenge Hook** — The "30 seconds to find a file" challenge is compelling and immediately relevant.
2. **"Name Game"** — The exercise of improving bad file names is engaging and practical.
3. **"Design Before You Build"** — Drawing folder structures on paper before implementing is a satisfying design exercise.
4. **Copy vs. Move Discovery** — The photocopy/tear analogy makes the distinction click.
5. **3-2-1 Rule** — The rule is memorable and empowering. Students feel they have a concrete strategy.
6. **"Teach Someone"** — Explaining concepts to a partner reinforces learning.

### Drop-off Risk
1. **Section 5 (Storage Management)** — If students don't have cloud storage or USB drives, the backup exercises may not work.
2. **Section 6 (Full Challenge)** — The10-minute time limit may cause students to rush and not learn properly.
3. **Lab Exercise 4 (Storage and Backup)** — "Upload one file to Google Drive, OneDrive, or another cloud service" assumes internet access and account creation.

### Confidence
- Before: 2/10 (most students don't think about file management)
- After: 6/10 (can name files, organize folders, understand backups)

### Skills Gained
- File system navigation
- File naming conventions
- Folder organization
- Copy, move, rename, delete operations
- 3-2-1 backup strategy
- Storage management basics

### Portfolio Value
The project produces a GitHub-ready file management system with README, naming guide, and before/after comparison. **Score: 8/10.**
