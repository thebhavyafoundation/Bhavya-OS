## Date: 2026-08-05

## Beginner Walkthrough

### Time Spent
- Lesson: ~2 hours (as designed)
- Project: ~1 week (as designed)
- Assessment: ~45 minutes
- Lab: ~45 minutes
- **Total: ~5 hours direct engagement + 1 week project**

### Confusing Concepts
1. **Desktop vs. Desktop Environment** — The distinction is explained but may be too abstract for beginners. The "Key Insight" paragraph introduces technical vocabulary ("desktop environment") that could overwhelm.
2. **CPU vs. Memory** — The car dashboard analogy helps, but the deeper explanation ("CPU is how fast your computer THINKS. Memory is how much it can HOLD at once") may still confuse students who have never thought about computer internals.
3. **Alt+Tab cycling** — The concept of "holding Alt while pressing Tab" is mechanically confusing for students who have never used modifier keys.

### Questions Asked
1. "What is the taskbar?" (covered in AI Mentor Sequence 1)
2. "What's the difference between minimize and close?" (covered in AI Mentor Sequence 2)
3. "What is Task Manager?" (covered in AI Mentor Sequence 3)
4. "Why should I use keyboard shortcuts?" (covered in AI Mentor Sequence 4)
5. "How do I change my wallpaper?" (covered in AI Mentor Sequence 5)
6. "Why does my computer have so many processes I don't recognize?"
7. "What happens if I close everything in Task Manager?"

### Points of Friction
1. **Section 1 opening** — "Your computer screen right now has dozens of elements on it. Most students can name maybe three." This assumes students are looking at a desktop. If they're on a phone or tablet, the lesson fails immediately.
2. **OS-specific instructions** — The lesson mentions Windows, macOS, and Linux but the instructions are primarily Windows-focused. Mac and Linux students may struggle.
3. **Settings Scavenger Hunt** — Challenge 4 asks students to find their IP address. This is significantly harder than the other challenges and may cause frustration.
4. **Task Manager** — The "Current State" step asks students to write down CPU/memory percentages. Beginners may not know what these numbers mean or whether they're "good" or "bad."
5. **Keyboard Shortcut Gauntlet** — 10 tasks with timing pressure may overwhelm beginners who are still learning to locate keys.

### Moments of Excitement
1. **Silent Discovery** — The "DO: Silent Discovery" section is excellent. Students genuinely enjoy exploring without instructions.
2. **Alt+Tab revelation** — Students are often amazed that Alt+Tab exists and saves time.
3. **CPU watching** — Seeing CPU/memory numbers change in real-time is engaging.
4. **Snap/Split-screen** — The visual satisfaction of arranging windows side-by-side is high.
5. **Speed run** — The keyboard shortcut speed run creates genuine excitement and competition.

### Drop-off Risk
1. **Section 3 (Settings)** — The Scavenger Hunt is the longest section and may lose students who can't find specific settings.
2. **Section 4 (Task Manager)** — The technical nature of CPU/memory monitoring may intimidate non-technical students.
3. **Section 5 (Keyboard Shortcuts)** — The sheer number of shortcuts (15+ in the reference table) may overwhelm.

### Confidence
- Before: 3/10 (typical Grade 9 student with basic computer use)
- After: 6/10 (can navigate, manage windows, use some shortcuts)

### Skills Gained
- Desktop navigation
- Window management (minimize, maximize, snap, Alt+Tab)
- Settings navigation
- Basic Task Manager usage
- 10+ keyboard shortcuts

### Portfolio Value
The project produces a GitHub-ready guide with screenshots, annotations, and reflection. A student who completes this would have a genuine portfolio piece. **Score: 8/10.**
