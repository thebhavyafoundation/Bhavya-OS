### Slide 3: Tokenization

LLMs don't read words — they read tokens.
"Hello, world!" → ["Hello", ",", " world", "!"]
This affects how AI understands text.

### Slide 4: Attention

When processing text, LLMs use attention to decide which words matter most.
This determines what the AI focuses on.

### Slide 5: Context Window

The context window is how much text an AI can "see" at once.
More context = more information.

### Slide 6: Why AI Makes Mistakes

LLMs don't "know" things — they predict tokens.
This leads to hallucinations.

### Slide 7: Better Prompts

Better: "Explain how neural networks learn."
Specificity matters.

## Reel Script

### Hook (0-3 seconds)

"How does ChatGPT actually work? Let me explain in 60 seconds."

### Body (3-45 seconds)

"LLMs don't read words — they read tokens. 'Hello, world!' becomes ['Hello', ',', ' world', '!'].

Then they use attention to decide which words matter. It's like asking: 'What am I looking for?' and 'What's available?'

The context window is how much text they can see. GPT-4 can see 128,000 tokens — that's 96,000 words.

But here's the thing: LLMs don't 'know' things. They predict tokens. That's why they sometimes make mistakes — hallucinations.

The fix? Write specific prompts. Instead of 'Tell me about AI,' say 'Explain how neural networks learn, focusing on training, backpropagation, and gradient descent.'"
