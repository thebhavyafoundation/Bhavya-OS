This Constitution establishes the founding philosophy, governance framework, principles, institutional structure, and operational direction of Bhavya Foundation.
It shall serve as the primary constitutional reference for all trustees, volunteers, officers, employees, advisors, committees, affiliated institutions, and future leadership of the Foundation.

PREAMBLE
We, the Founder and the Founding Trustees of Bhavya Foundation, establish this institution with a solemn commitment to serve humanity through environmental stewardship, education, preservation of cultural heritage, and community development.
We recognize that civilizations endure not merely because of wealth or power, but because generations choose to protect the natural world, cultivate knowledge, and preserve the values that bind communities together.
Bhavya Foundation is established as a public charitable institution devoted to the common good. It exists to restore forests, expand access to knowledge, encourage responsible innovation, strengthen communities, and preserve heritage with integrity and respect.
The Foundation shall conduct its activities lawfully, transparently, and independently. It shall not exist for personal enrichment, partisan political purposes, or private ownership. Its assets, reputation, and resources shall always be dedicated to charitable purposes and the public interest.
Every person who serves Bhavya Foundation—whether as Founder, Trustee, volunteer, advisor, employee, or partner—accepts the responsibility to protect its mission and uphold its values.
This Constitution is adopted as the guiding charter of Bhavya Foundation so that its purpose may continue with clarity, integrity, and accountability for generations to come.

ARTICLE 6
FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES
Section 6.1 — Purpose
The Foundational Principles constitute the moral and constitutional framework of Bhavya Foundation. Every decision, program me, partnership, policy, and action undertaken by the Foundation shall be interpreted in harmony with these principles.
Where uncertainty exists, these principles shall guide interpretation.

Principle I
Stewardship Before Ownership
Bhavya Foundation exists as a steward of public trust.
No individual, including the Founder, Trustees, officers, volunteers, employees, or donors, shall regard the Foundation or its assets as personal property.
Every person serving the Foundation acts as a temporary guardian of an institution created for the benefit of society.
The Foundation shall always prioritize long-term public benefit over short-term institutional convenience.

Principle II
Nature Before Exploitation
The Foundation recognizes nature not merely as a resource but as the foundation of human civilization.
Accordingly, the Foundation shall promote:
ecological restoration;
biodiversity conservation;
protection of native species;
sustainable resource management;
climate resilience; and
environmental education.
The Foundation shall encourage development that respects ecological balance and minimizes environmental harm.

Principle III
Knowledge as a Public Good
Knowledge is fundamental to human dignity and social progress.
The Foundation shall work to expand equitable access to education, digital literacy, scientific understanding, responsible artificial intelligence, and lifelong learning.
Knowledge created, preserved, or disseminated by the Foundation should, wherever lawful and appropriate, be shared in ways that maximize public benefit.

Principle IV
Heritage as Living Responsibility
The Foundation recognizes that cultural heritage, traditional knowledge, languages, sacred places, and community traditions are valuable public assets.
Where it undertakes heritage-related work, the Foundation shall do so respectfully, in accordance with applicable laws, with appropriate permissions, and in consultation with relevant communities and authorities.
Preservation shall seek to protect authenticity while serving present and future generations.

Principle V
Service Above Recognition
The Foundation exists to serve.
Public recognition, awards, publicity, or institutional prestige shall never become objectives in themselves.
The value of the Foundation shall be measured by its positive contribution to society rather than by public attention.

Principle VI
Integrity Without Compromise
Integrity shall remain the Foundation's highest institutional standard.
All representatives shall conduct themselves honestly, lawfully, professionally, and ethically.
No objective of the Foundation shall justify unlawful or unethical conduct.

Principle VII
Transparency Builds Trust
Public confidence depends upon transparency.
The Foundation shall maintain accurate records of governance, finances, programs, and decisions.
Subject to legal, privacy, and confidentiality requirements, the Foundation shall strive to communicate openly with beneficiaries, volunteers, partners, and donors.

Principle VIII
Independence
The Foundation shall remain institutionally independent.
It may collaborate with governments, educational institutions, corporations, civil society organizations, and community groups where such collaboration advances the Foundation's mission and complies with law.
Such collaboration shall not compromise the Foundation's independence, integrity, or charitable purpose.

Principle IX
Excellence Through Discipline
The Foundation shall pursue excellence through planning, accountability, continuous learning, and disciplined execution.
Projects should be designed with measurable objectives, responsible management, and regular evaluation.

Principle X
Innovation With Responsibility
Innovation is encouraged where it serves the public good.
The Foundation shall promote responsible research and the ethical use of emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence.
Innovation shall be guided by human dignity, safety, fairness, transparency, and respect for applicable laws.

Principle XI
Respect for Human Dignity
Every individual shall be treated with respect.
The Foundation shall not discriminate on grounds prohibited by applicable law.
Its programs should strive to be inclusive and accessible.

Principle XII
Accountability
Every office-bearer, trustee, employee, volunteer, consultant, and partner is accountable for actions undertaken on behalf of the Foundation.
Authority shall always be accompanied by responsibility.
Decisions shall be documented and subject to appropriate review.

Principle XIII
Long-Term Institutional Stewardship
The Foundation shall make decisions with consideration for future generations.
Projects, infrastructure, finances, intellectual property, and institutional knowledge shall be managed in a manner that supports the Foundation's continuity and resilience.

Principle XIV
Collaboration
The Foundation recognizes that meaningful public benefit is often achieved through collaboration.
It shall seek constructive partnerships with educational institutions, community organizations, public authorities, researchers, philanthropic organizations, and responsible private-sector entities where such partnerships further its charitable objectives.

Principle XV
Continuous Learning
The Foundation shall remain a learning institution.
Policies, programs, and practices should be reviewed periodically in light of evidence, research, experience, and changing societal needs.
Learning shall strengthen—not weaken—the Foundation's core principles.

Section 6.2 — Constitutional Effect
These Foundational Principles shall guide:
interpretation of this Constitution;
drafting of Foundation policies;
governance decisions;
appointment of trustees and officers;
strategic planning;
program me development; and
partnerships and collaborations.
No subordinate policy or internal guideline should conflict with these principles.

PART IV
GOVERNANCE
ARTICLE 8
THE BOARD OF TRUSTEES

Section 8.1 — Establishment
The Board of Trustees ("the Board") is the principal governing body of Bhavya Foundation.
The Board shall govern the Foundation in accordance with:
this Constitution;
the Trust Deed;
applicable laws;
resolutions of the Board; and
the charitable objectives of the Foundation.
The Board shall exercise its authority solely in the public interest and for the advancement of the Foundation's mission.

Section 8.2 — Role of the Board
The Board shall provide strategic oversight, fiduciary supervision, institutional stewardship, and policy governance.
The Board shall not manage day-to-day operations except where necessary or where executive authority has not yet been delegated.

Section 8.3 — Composition
The Foundation shall initially consist of three members of the Board:
Founder & Managing Trustee
Shri Manohar Lal

Trustee
Smt. Kanta Devi

Trustee
Shri Kuldeep Sangal

The Board may be expanded in the future in accordance with this Constitution and the Trust Deed.
The preferred long-term size of the Board shall ordinarily be five, seven, or nine Trustees, unless otherwise determined by amendment or applicable law.

Section 8.4 — Eligibility
A person may be appointed as Trustee only if the Board is satisfied that the individual:
possesses integrity and good character;
supports the charitable mission of the Foundation;
is capable of exercising independent judgment;
is willing to devote adequate time to governance responsibilities;
has no disqualifying legal restrictions; and
agrees to comply with the Constitution, Trust Deed, and Foundation policies.
The Foundation shall strive to appoint Trustees with diverse expertise, including but not limited to education, environmental conservation, finance, law, governance, technology, community development, and public service.

Section 8.5 — Fiduciary Duty
Every Trustee owes a fiduciary duty to the Foundation.
Each Trustee shall:
act honestly;
act in good faith;
avoid conflicts of interest;
exercise reasonable care and diligence;
protect Foundation assets;
maintain confidentiality where appropriate; and
place the interests of the Foundation above personal interests.

Section 8.6 — Collective Responsibility
The Board functions collectively.
No individual Trustee, other than where expressly authorized by this Constitution, the Trust Deed, or a Board resolution, shall act on behalf of the Foundation.

Section 8.7 — Powers of the Board
Subject to this Constitution and applicable law, the Board may:
approve annual strategic plans;
approve budgets;
approve annual reports;
appoint committees;
appoint executive officers;
establish institutions, programs, and projects;
acquire or dispose of property for charitable purposes;
approve major partnerships;
approve financial policies;
approve governance policies;
authorize bank signatories;
oversee compliance;
protect the Foundation's reputation.

Section 8.8 — Duties of Trustees
Trustees shall:
attend Board meetings regularly;
review governance documents before meetings;
participate constructively in deliberations;
monitor institutional performance;
encourage ethical conduct;
protect donor confidence;
support transparency;
avoid political misuse of the Foundation.

Section 8.9 — Independence
Trustees shall exercise independent judgment.
No Trustee shall accept instructions from any political party, private interest, donor, corporation, or external organization that conflict with the Foundation's charitable purpose or legal obligations.

Section 8.10 — Meetings
The Board shall ordinarily meet at least four times each calendar year.
Additional meetings may be convened by the Managing Trustee or as otherwise provided by the Trust Deed.
Notice of meetings should ordinarily include:
date;
time;
venue or virtual platform;
agenda; and
relevant papers.

Section 8.11 — Quorum
No Board meeting shall transact business unless a quorum is present.
Until the Board is expanded, all three Trustees should ordinarily be present for decisions of significant importance.
Routine matters may be handled in accordance with procedures adopted by the Board.

Section 8.12 — Decision Making
The Board shall strive to reach decisions by consensus.
Where consensus cannot reasonably be achieved, decisions may be made by vote in accordance with the Trust Deed and applicable law.
Major constitutional, financial, or strategic matters should receive broad support from the Board.

Section 8.13 — Confidentiality
Trustees shall maintain confidentiality regarding information that is legally privileged, commercially sensitive, or confidential by nature, except where disclosure is required by law or authorized by the Board.

Section 8.14 — Continuing Education
Trustees are encouraged to remain informed regarding:
nonprofit governance;
environmental stewardship;
charitable law;
financial oversight;
technology;
safeguarding;
ethics; and
leadership.
The Foundation may provide orientation and ongoing governance training.

Section 8.15 — Vacancies
Where a vacancy arises, the Board shall seek to appoint a suitably qualified individual in accordance with the Trust Deed and the Foundation's governance policies.
Appointments should prioritize competence, integrity, and commitment to the Foundation's mission.

Section 8.16 — Removal
A Trustee may be removed only in accordance with:
the Trust Deed;
principles of procedural fairness;
applicable law; and
the Foundation's governance policies.
Grounds may include serious misconduct, incapacity, persistent failure to discharge duties, or material breach of fiduciary obligations.

Section 8.17 — Remuneration
Trustees shall ordinarily serve without salary.
The Board may authorize reimbursement of reasonable, documented expenses incurred in carrying out Foundation duties, consistent with applicable law and Foundation policy.

Section 8.18 — Duty to Future Generations
Every Trustee shall remember that they are not merely managing present-day activities.
They are custodians of an institution intended to serve generations through responsible governance, prudent stewardship, and unwavering commitment to the Foundation's constitutional values.

CONSTITUTIONAL POWERS, RESERVED MATTERS, AND DELEGATED AUTHORITY

Section 10.1 — Purpose
This Article establishes the constitutional allocation of authority within Bhavya Foundation.
Authority exists to enable effective governance, protect the Foundation's mission, and ensure accountability.
No office shall exercise powers beyond those granted by this Constitution, the Trust Deed, Board resolutions, or applicable law.

CHAPTER I
Constitutional Allocation of Authority
The Foundation shall exercise authority through the following constitutional organs:
Founder & Steward
Board of Trustees
Managing Trustee
Standing Committees
Executive Leadership
Authorized Officers
Each organ shall perform distinct functions while cooperating in the public interest.

CHAPTER II
Reserved Matters
The following matters are reserved exclusively for the Board of Trustees and shall not be delegated unless expressly permitted by law:
Approval of the annual budget.
Approval of audited financial statements.
Adoption or amendment of major Foundation policies.
Appointment or removal of Trustees in accordance with the Trust Deed.
Appointment of the Executive Director.
Creation of permanent institutions under the Foundation.
Acquisition or disposal of significant assets.
Borrowing or assuming significant financial obligations.
Approval of annual strategic plans.
Recommendation of constitutional amendments.
Dissolution or merger of the Foundation, if ever considered.

CHAPTER III
Powers of the Founder & Steward
Subject to this Constitution and applicable law, the Founder & Steward may:
Recommend long-term institutional strategy.
Recommend new charitable programmes.
Recommend establishment of centres, institutes, and initiatives.
Recommend appointments for constitutional offices.
Recommend strategic partnerships.
Encourage innovation.
Request that matters of constitutional significance be placed before the Board.
The Founder shall exercise these powers with humility, transparency, and in the best interests of the Foundation.

CHAPTER IV
Powers of the Managing Trustee
The Managing Trustee shall have authority to:
Supervise day-to-day administration.
Implement Board resolutions.
Coordinate Foundation programmes.
Authorize routine operational expenditure within Board-approved limits.
Supervise employees and volunteers.
Represent the Foundation in routine administrative matters.
Execute contracts authorized by the Board.
Maintain institutional discipline.

CHAPTER V
Delegated Authority
The Board may delegate administrative authority through written resolutions.
Delegation shall:
specify the scope of authority;
identify the responsible office;
define reporting obligations; and
remain subject to Board oversight.
Delegation of authority shall never remove accountability.

CHAPTER VI
Financial Authority
Financial authority shall be exercised prudently.
The Board shall establish written financial approval limits.
Routine expenditures may be authorized by designated office-bearers within approved limits.
Material expenditures shall require Board approval in accordance with financial policies.
No person shall approve a payment to themselves without an independent approval process.

CHAPTER VII
Institutional Safeguards
The Foundation shall establish safeguards to prevent:
misuse of authority;
financial misconduct;
conflicts of interest;
unauthorized commitments;
misuse of Foundation property;
reputational harm; and
mission drift.
Every office-bearer shall support these safeguards.

CHAPTER VIII
Separation of Governance and Management
The Board governs.
Management executes.
Trustees shall avoid unnecessary involvement in routine administration.
Management shall remain accountable to the Board.
This distinction promotes effective governance and operational efficiency.

CHAPTER IX
Emergency Authority
Where immediate action is necessary to protect life, safety, Foundation property, or legal compliance, the Managing Trustee may take reasonable temporary action.
Such action shall be reported to the Board at the earliest practical opportunity.

CHAPTER X
Institutional Records
Every exercise of constitutional authority relating to major governance, financial, legal, or strategic matters shall be documented.
The Foundation shall maintain accurate records of:
Board resolutions;
delegated authorities;
major contracts;
policy approvals;
strategic decisions; and
constitutional amendments.

CHAPTER XI
Accountability
Every constitutional office shall remain accountable to the Foundation.
Authority is granted for service, not privilege.
Failure to exercise authority responsibly may result in review or other action consistent with this Constitution, the Trust Deed, and applicable law.

CHAPTER XII
Interpretation
Where uncertainty exists regarding the exercise of authority:
the Constitution shall prevail;
the Trust Deed shall be read consistently with the Constitution to the extent permitted by law;
Board resolutions shall guide implementation; and
the Foundational Principles shall inform interpretation.

PART VIII
ARTICLE 12
FINANCIAL STEWARDSHIP, TRANSPARENCY, AND PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Section 12.1 — Constitutional Principle
Bhavya Foundation recognizes that every financial resource entrusted to it is held in trust for the public benefit.
The Foundation shall manage its finances with integrity, prudence, transparency, accountability, and in accordance with applicable law.
Financial decisions shall always support the Foundation's charitable objectives and long-term sustainability.

Section 12.2 — Nature of Foundation Assets
All assets of the Foundation, whether movable, immovable, financial, intellectual, or digital, shall belong exclusively to the Foundation.
No Founder, Trustee, office-bearer, volunteer, employee, donor, or other individual shall acquire ownership rights over Foundation assets by virtue of their position or service.
Foundation assets shall be used solely for lawful charitable purposes.

Section 12.3 — Sources of Funds
The Foundation may lawfully receive financial or in-kind support through:
voluntary donations;
grants;
corporate social responsibility (CSR) contributions, where eligible;
government grants consistent with the Foundation's mission;
philanthropic institutions;
charitable fundraising;
educational programmes;
publications;
investment income, where permitted by law; and
other lawful sources.
The Foundation shall not knowingly accept funds derived from unlawful activities.

Section 12.4 — Acceptance of Donations
The Foundation shall exercise due diligence before accepting donations.
It may decline any donation that:
conflicts with the Foundation's mission;
compromises institutional independence;
imposes unlawful conditions;
creates an unacceptable conflict of interest; or
may reasonably damage the Foundation's integrity or reputation.
Acceptance of donations shall not entitle any donor to control the governance or charitable activities of the Foundation.

Section 12.5 — Financial Planning
The Foundation shall prepare an annual budget.
The budget should:
align with strategic priorities;
support responsible financial management;
encourage sustainability; and
provide for prudent reserves where appropriate.
Budgets shall be reviewed and approved in accordance with the governance framework.

Section 12.6 — Accounting
The Foundation shall maintain complete, accurate, and timely books of account.
Financial records shall:
comply with applicable accounting standards and laws;
accurately reflect all receipts, expenditures, assets, liabilities, and restricted funds;
be retained for the period required by law and Foundation policy.

Section 12.7 — Banking
Foundation funds shall be deposited only in accounts held in the name of the Foundation.
The Board shall establish authorized signatory arrangements and internal financial controls.
No Foundation bank account shall be maintained in the personal name of any individual.

Section 12.8 — Procurement
Goods and services shall be procured in a manner that is:
fair;
transparent;
competitive where appropriate;
value-for-money;
free from improper influence.
Procurement decisions shall prioritize quality, integrity, sustainability, and responsible use of charitable resources.

Section 12.9 — Financial Controls
The Foundation shall maintain written financial control procedures, including:
approval authorities;
payment controls;
segregation of duties where practical;
asset management;
inventory management;
documentation standards;
fraud prevention measures.
No individual shall exercise unrestricted control over Foundation finances.

Section 12.10 — Audit
The Foundation shall arrange for audits where required by law and may undertake additional independent reviews as appropriate.
Audit findings shall be considered by the Board, and reasonable corrective actions shall be implemented where necessary.

Section 12.11 — Transparency
The Foundation shall strive to communicate openly regarding its charitable activities and financial stewardship.
Subject to legal and privacy obligations, the Foundation should publish:
annual reports;
audited financial statements, where applicable;
programme summaries;
impact reports; and
other information that promotes public confidence.

Section 12.12 — Restricted Funds
Where donations are accepted for a specific charitable purpose, the Foundation shall make reasonable efforts to apply those funds consistently with the donor's lawful intent.
If fulfilment becomes impossible or impracticable, the Foundation shall manage such funds in accordance with applicable law and sound charitable governance.

Section 12.13 — Investment of Funds
Where permitted by law, the Foundation may invest surplus funds prudently.
Investment decisions shall prioritize:
preservation of capital;
lawful conduct;
responsible risk management; and
long-term institutional sustainability.
Speculative or high-risk investment strategies inconsistent with prudent stewardship should be avoided.

Section 12.14 — Financial Reserves
The Foundation may establish reserves to support:
continuity of operations;
emergency response;
maintenance of assets;
strategic initiatives; and
long-term resilience.
Reserve policies shall be approved by the Board.

Section 12.15 — Conflict of Interest in Financial Matters
Any individual with a material personal interest in a financial decision shall:
disclose the interest promptly;
refrain from participating in the relevant decision-making process where required; and
comply with the Foundation's Conflict of Interest Policy.

Section 12.16 — Misuse of Resources
The misuse, misappropriation, unauthorized use, or diversion of Foundation assets is strictly prohibited.
The Foundation shall investigate credible allegations and take appropriate action consistent with applicable law, procedural fairness, and its governance policies.

Section 12.17 — Constitutional Commitment
Every financial decision shall reflect the understanding that the Foundation administers resources entrusted to it for the benefit of society.
Financial stewardship is therefore a constitutional responsibility rather than merely an administrative function.

Constitutional Statement
Every rupee entrusted to Bhavya Foundation represents the trust of a donor, the hope of a beneficiary, and the responsibility of an institution. We shall administer those resources with honesty, diligence, transparency, and respect for the public good.

reasons supporting the amendment;
expected institutional impact; and
an assessment of consistency with the Foundation's constitutional philosophy.

supports the Foundation's charitable purpose;
complies with applicable law;
preserves institutional integrity;

Knowledge
Heritage



Bhavya Foundation is established with the intention of serving society as a permanent public charitable institution.
The Foundation shall strive to maintain continuity through responsible governance, prudent financial stewardship, leadership development, and faithful adherence to this Constitution.
Every generation of leadership shall preserve and strengthen the Foundation for those who follow.

Section 16.2 — Institutional Independence
The Foundation shall remain independent in the exercise of its charitable mission.
While it may collaborate with governments, educational institutions, civil society organizations, philanthropic institutions, and private entities, no such relationship shall compromise:
the constitutional identity of the Foundation;
its charitable purpose;
its integrity;
or its independence.

The dissolution of Bhavya Foundation shall be regarded as an extraordinary measure and shall occur only:
in accordance with applicable law;
in accordance with the Trust Deed;
after careful consideration by the Board of Trustees; and

the Founder;
Trustees;
employees;
volunteers;
donors; or
any private individual.

This Constitution is the supreme internal governance document of Bhavya Foundation.
Every policy, manual, charter, operational procedure, committee rule, and internal guideline shall be interpreted consistently with this Constitution.

This Constitution shall be interpreted:
in good faith;
in favour of charitable purpose;
consistently with the Foundational Principles;
consistently with applicable law; and
with due regard for the long-term interests of the Foundation.

The authoritative version of this Constitution shall be the English version adopted by the Foundation.

the original Constitution;
all constitutional amendments;
the Founder's Declaration;
the Trust Deed;
annual reports;
institutional history;
historical records; and
documents of enduring constitutional significance.

Section 16.9 — Constitutional Review
The Board shall periodically review this Constitution to ensure:
continued legal compliance;
effective governance;
institutional resilience;
and faithful implementation.

This Constitution shall take effect upon its formal adoption by the Founding Board of Trustees and shall remain in force until amended in accordance with this Constitution, the Trust Deed, and applicable law.
